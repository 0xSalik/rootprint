\section{The Big Idea}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{blackbox}[One sentence]
\textbf{Rootprint gives every Kashmiri craft product a living digital passport: the artisan speaks, AI structures the knowledge, the system creates a tamper-aware QR identity, and the buyer can scan, verify, listen, ask questions, and trust the product instantly.}
\end{blackbox}

Rootprint is not another marketplace. It is not another tourism listing. It is not a generic AI chatbot.

\tagline{Rootprint is the proof infrastructure for Kashmiri craft.}

Today, a fake ``Kashmiri pashmina'' can use the same words as a real one: handmade, authentic, heritage, pure, original. The buyer sees a label. The artisan has a lifetime of knowledge. There is no bridge between the two.

Rootprint builds that bridge.

The product begins with one simple action: an artisan, shop owner, cooperative, or craft student records a spoken story about a product or craft process. Rootprint converts that conversation into a structured \textbf{Craft Passport} containing origin, technique, material, lineage, media evidence, and a public QR page. The QR code travels with the product as its digital identity.

\begin{goldbox}[The winning frame]
\textbf{If Kashmir is globally known for craft, Kashmir should also own the digital standard for proving craft authenticity. Rootprint is that standard.}
\end{goldbox}

\section{The Problem}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{The Trust Gap}

The craft market has a brutal information problem. The people with the deepest knowledge have the least digital proof. The people selling imitations often have better packaging, better English, better SEO, and better access to buyers.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Buyers cannot verify authenticity.} A tourist, online buyer, retailer, or collector cannot easily know whether a product is handmade, where it came from, or who made it.
  \item \textbf{Artisans cannot carry their story with the product.} Once a shawl, carpet, box, or carved object leaves the workshop, the maker's identity disappears.
  \item \textbf{Knowledge is trapped in memory.} Techniques, motifs, family histories, tools, material choices, and process details are rarely captured in structured form.
  \item \textbf{Middlemen benefit from opacity.} When proof is missing, margins flow to whoever controls the buyer relationship, not necessarily the maker.
  \item \textbf{Fake products dilute the Kashmir brand.} Every counterfeit product weakens the premium that genuine Kashmiri artisans should command.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{redbox}[The emotional truth]
Kashmiri craft does not only need more buyers. It needs memory, identity, and proof that can travel with the product.
\end{redbox}

\section{The Product: Rootprint Craft Passport}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{What the User Experiences}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The artisan speaks.} They record a short voice note or interview in Kashmiri, Urdu, Hindi, or English.
  \item \textbf{Rootprint understands.} AI transcribes, translates, and extracts craft-specific facts into a structured record.
  \item \textbf{The record is reviewed.} The artisan/team can correct names, places, techniques, materials, and evidence.
  \item \textbf{The passport is minted.} Rootprint generates a unique Passport ID, QR code, score, and public page.
  \item \textbf{The buyer scans.} The product page opens instantly, showing the maker, process, evidence, and story.
  \item \textbf{The buyer asks.} A mini AI guide answers questions from the verified record: ``Why is this expensive?'', ``What is kani weaving?'', ``How do I know this is handmade?''
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What a Craft Passport Contains}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Artisan or business identity
  \item Craft category
  \item Village, district, or craft cluster
  \item Product details
  \item Technique names
  \item Tools used
  \item Material provenance
  \item Family/apprenticeship lineage
  \item Process summary
  \item Audio transcript excerpt
  \item Photos or short video evidence
  \item Passport completeness score
  \item Evidence level
  \item QR code and public URL
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}

\begin{goldbox}[The product metaphor]
For buyers, Rootprint feels like scanning a museum label, certificate, and maker story at the same time. For artisans, it feels like speaking once and receiving a professional digital identity.
\end{goldbox}

\section{The ``Head Spin'' Features}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The MVP should not be a basic CRUD app with QR codes. It needs two or three moments that make judges sit forward.

\subsection{1. Voice-to-Passport in Front of the Jury}

The team records a 60--90 second artisan-style answer live:

\begin{quote}
``My name is \ldots I am from \ldots This is a handwoven pashmina shawl. We use \ldots The technique came from \ldots The material is sourced from \ldots''
\end{quote}

Rootprint turns it into a polished Craft Passport with structured fields, story text, score, and QR code.

\begin{blackbox}
\textbf{Judge reaction target:} ``They created an authenticity page from speech in under two minutes.''
\end{blackbox}

\subsection{2. Ask the Craft: Buyer AI on the QR Page}

After scanning the QR page, the judge can ask questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item ``Why is this product valuable?''
  \item ``What technique was used?''
  \item ``What evidence supports the claim?''
  \item ``Explain this to a tourist in simple words.''
  \item ``Generate a luxury product description for an international buyer.''
\end{itemize}

The AI answers only from the passport record, making the product page interactive and trustworthy.

\begin{goldbox}[Why this is powerful]
Most QR certificates are dead pages. Rootprint's passport is conversational. The product can explain itself.
\end{goldbox}

\subsection{3. Fake-vs-Rootprint Scan Moment}

Show two product cards:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A generic tag saying ``Authentic Kashmiri Handmade'' with no Rootprint QR.
  \item A Rootprint tag with a QR code and Passport ID.
\end{enumerate}

When the first is scanned or searched, the app shows: \textbf{No verifiable Rootprint found. Ask the seller for proof.}

When the second is scanned, it opens the full passport.

\begin{blackbox}
\textbf{Judge reaction target:} ``This could change consumer behavior. People will start asking for Rootprint.''
\end{blackbox}

\subsection{4. Evidence Score, Not Blind Certification}

The system does not make reckless legal claims. It shows a transparent score:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Identity provided
  \item Location provided
  \item Technique specificity
  \item Material detail
  \item Media evidence
  \item Human review
  \item Community/document reference
\end{itemize}

This makes the product credible. Judges will respect that the team knows the difference between AI claims and verified evidence.

\subsection{5. Kashmir Craft Knowledge Graph}

Behind every passport, Rootprint stores structured craft knowledge. Over time, this becomes a living graph:

\begin{center}
\textbf{Artisan} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Craft} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Technique} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Material} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Region} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Product} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Buyer Trust}
\end{center}

The hackathon can show a simple graph view: multiple passports connected by craft, district, material, and technique. This is the seed of a defensible data moat.

\section{The Final Pitch in 90 Seconds}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{blackbox}
Kashmir has one of the strongest craft identities in the world, but the proof of that craft is still fragile. A real artisan may spend years mastering a technique, but at the point of sale, their product often competes with a fake using the same words: authentic, handmade, Kashmiri.

Rootprint is the trust layer for Kashmiri craft. An artisan speaks their story in their own language. Our AI turns that oral knowledge into a structured Craft Passport: who made it, where it came from, what technique was used, what material was used, and what evidence supports the claim. The system generates a QR code that travels with the product. A buyer scans it and can not only read the story, but ask the product questions.

We are starting with pashmina because the counterfeit problem is obvious and the global value is high. But the passport model expands to carpets, papier-mache, walnut wood, embroidery, copperware, tourism, export, and retail integrations.

Rootprint is not a marketplace. It is the proof layer underneath the market. It makes Kashmiri craft impossible to erase and harder to fake.
\end{blackbox}
